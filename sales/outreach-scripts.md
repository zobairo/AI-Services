# Outreach Scripts

The daily 10–15 touches from [docs/04](../docs/04-go-to-market.md) and [docs/06](../docs/06-roadmap-90-days.md). Rules that apply to every message below:

1. **Two minutes of research per prospect, minimum.** The specific detail in line one is the whole message. Without it you are spam.
2. **One ask, and make it small.** "Worth a look?" beats "Can we schedule a 30-minute discovery call?"
3. **Short.** Under 90 words. It will be read on a phone, between jobs.
4. **No AI jargon.** Never "LLM", "RAG", "agentic". They buy answered calls, not architecture.
5. **Send the demo, don't describe it.** A 60-second video of their own business being served beats any paragraph.

---

## Cold email — the demo-first opener

> **Subject:** your after-hours calls
>
> Hi <name>,
>
> I called <business> on Saturday to check something and got the answerphone — which is normal for a <clinic/garage/salon>, but it made me curious how many bookings that costs you.
>
> I build assistants for <industry> that answer the phone and WhatsApp when nobody can, take the booking, and text you a summary. I put together a 90-second video showing it answering questions using your own prices from your website: <link>
>
> If it looks useful, I'll show you how it works on a quick call. If not, no hard feelings — you can keep the video.
>
> <your name>
> <phone>

**Why it works:** a real observation, a demo already built, and an exit that costs them nothing.

## The 3-touch follow-up sequence

Most replies come from touch 2 and 3. Not following up is the most expensive habit in this business.

**Touch 2 — +3 days. Add proof, not pressure.**

> Hi <name> — following up on the demo I sent. One <industry> client went from missing about 30 calls a week to missing none; they booked 11 extra appointments in the first month. Same setup would take about a week for you. Want me to walk you through it?

**Touch 3 — +4 days. The breakup message (highest reply rate of the three).**

> Hi <name> — I'll stop here, I know you're busy. If answering after-hours calls ever moves up your list, just reply to this email and I'll pick it back up. The demo link stays live either way: <link>

Then genuinely stop. Add them to a "revisit in 3 months" list.

## Warm network message (send these first, on day one)

> Hi <name>, hope you're well. I've started building AI automations for small businesses — the practical kind: answering customer questions on WhatsApp, following up leads, handling paperwork. Do you know anyone running a <clinic/agency/shop> who's drowning in that stuff? Happy to build them a free demo so they can see it working. No pressure either way.

**Warm intros convert roughly ten times better than cold.** Exhaust this list before spending a day on cold outreach.

## Upwork / freelance proposal

Never open with "I am an experienced AI developer." Open with their problem and a demo.

> Hi <name>,
>
> You want <restate their need in one plain sentence>. I've built exactly this <N> times — here's one running live: <demo link>.
>
> For your case I'd:
> 1. <Concrete step from their brief>
> 2. <Concrete step>
> 3. <Test it against 15 real questions from your customers before calling it done>
>
> Timeline: <X> days. Price: $<amount> fixed, half up front, half when it passes the tests you approve.
>
> One question: <a specific, intelligent question about their brief that shows you read it>.
>
> <your name>

**Send 5 per day, every day, in months 1–3.** Answer the question at the end and you have started a conversation instead of a bid.

## The 20-minute discovery call

**Minutes 0–2 — set the frame.**
> "Thanks for the time. I'd like to understand how you handle <the process> today, then I'll tell you honestly whether this is worth automating. If it isn't, I'll say so."

**Minutes 2–12 — ask, and shut up.**
- Walk me through what happens when a new customer messages you.
- Who handles that, and how long does it take them?
- What happens after hours or when everyone's busy?
- How many of those do you get in a week?
- What have you tried already? What went wrong with it?
- If this worked perfectly, what would change for you?

Write down their exact words. Those words go into your proposal.

**Minutes 12–17 — show the demo.** Their content, on screen, working. Say nothing while it answers the first question.

**Minutes 17–20 — close to the next step.**
> "Based on what you've told me, I'd build <one-sentence solution>. That's about <X> weeks and $<amount> to build, then $<amount> a month to run and maintain. I'll send a one-page proposal today with the exact list of questions it has to pass before you pay the balance. Sound fair?"

Then be quiet and let them answer.

## Handling the four objections you will hear

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | "Compared to what it saves, or compared to your budget right now? If it's budget, we can start with just <smallest piece> for $<less> and add the rest when it's paying for itself." |
| "Will it say something wrong to a customer?" | "It only answers from your documents, and it hands off to your team whenever it's unsure — I'll show you exactly where that line sits. Before launch it has to pass a list of real questions that you approve." |
| "We'll do it ourselves with ChatGPT." | "You can, and for simple stuff you should. The work is connecting it to your calendar and WhatsApp, keeping it correct as prices change, and making sure it doesn't invent answers. That's the part I maintain." |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to — one question first so I send the right thing: is the bigger problem the missed calls or the follow-up afterwards?" *(Turns a brush-off back into a conversation.)* |
