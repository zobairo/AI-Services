# Go-To-Market & Sales

Technology does not get you clients. This document is the actual job for the first 90 days.

## Pick a niche (really)

"AI automation for everyone" fails because your message is generic, your demos are generic, and every project is custom. Pick **one niche, maximum two**, using three criteria:

1. **Repetitive document or communication work** (the pain AI actually solves today)
2. **Money to spend** (they already pay staff or agencies for this work)
3. **Your access** (you know someone in it, worked in it, or can physically walk into their offices)

Six beginner-friendly niches and the specific pain to lead with (catalog codes from the [Services Catalog](02-services-catalog.md)):

| Niche | Painful process they'll pay to automate | Matching service | Later: package |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real-estate agencies | Lead follow-up within minutes + listing descriptions | B3 + B4 | [E1](08-industry-packages.md) |
| E-commerce brands | Same 20 support questions, order status, returns | A1 | [E3](08-industry-packages.md) |
| Recruitment agencies | CV screening against job specs, candidate outreach | C3 + B2 | custom |
| Clinics / dental | Missed calls, appointment requests, reminders, FAQs | A1 + A5, later A2 | [E2](08-industry-packages.md) |
| Restaurants / hospitality | Unanswered phones, reviews, weekly socials | A2 + A7 | [E4](08-industry-packages.md) |
| Marketing / web agencies | Client reporting, content drafts (white-label) | C7 + B4 | [E6](08-industry-packages.md) |

In Phase 1 you sell single services from this table. From Phase 3, the [industry packages](08-industry-packages.md) become the lead offer — same niches, bundled pitch, higher deal size.

## Sell the outcome, not the technology

Nobody buys "n8n workflows with Claude API." They buy:

- ❌ "I build AI chatbots with RAG"
- ✅ **"Your website answers every customer in 10 seconds, 24/7, in their language — and books the appointment."**
- ❌ "I automate document processing"
- ✅ **"Your invoices enter the accounting system by themselves. Your team stops typing."**

Write one outcome sentence per service for your niche. That sentence is your website headline, your outreach opener, and your demo intro.

## The demo-first sales motion

Your unfair advantage as a technical founder: **build the demo before asking for money.**

1. Pick a real prospect. Spend 30–60 minutes building a rough version of the automation *on their real content* (their website FAQ, their listing data, a public form of theirs).
2. Record a 3–5 minute Loom: "I built this for you already. Here it is answering your actual customers' questions."
3. Send it. Offer a 20-minute call to hand it over / go deeper.

A prospect watching an AI answer *their* customers' questions with *their* data converts far better than any proposal PDF. Cost to you: an hour, thanks to your template library.

## Channels, ranked for a solo founder with no audience

1. **Warm network first.** Message every business owner you know personally. Script: "I've started building AI automations for [niche]. Who do you know that drowns in [pain]? Happy to show a working demo." Warm intros convert 10× cold.
2. **Upwork / freelance platforms** — your cash-and-reviews engine for months 1–3. Filter for "AI chatbot", "automation", "n8n", "document extraction". 5–10 tailored proposals/day, lead with a relevant Loom demo. Margins are worse; treat it as paid lead-gen and portfolio building, then reduce dependency.
3. **Direct outreach (LinkedIn / email / WhatsApp)** in your niche. Sustainable solo routine: **10–15 personalized touches/day**, 3-touch sequence: (1) one-line pain observation + demo Loom, (2) +3 days: a concrete result ("this saved a similar agency 11 h/week"), (3) +4 days: short breakup message. Personalized means you looked at their business for 2 minutes, not mail-merge spam.
4. **Local businesses.** Walk in / call. Zero competition from global freelancers, trust from being local and in-person. Clinics and agencies especially.
5. **Agency partnerships.** Web/marketing agencies constantly get asked for "AI stuff" they can't build. Offer white-label delivery at 60–70% of your direct price. One good agency = recurring deal flow.
6. **Content (later).** Short screen-recordings of real automations working. Compounds, but slowly — don't let it eat selling time in months 1–3.

## Pipeline math (plan for it, don't be surprised by it)

Typical cold-ish numbers: **100 contacted → ~10 replies → ~4 calls → 1 client.** Warm network and demo-first improve this a lot; still, plan volume assuming the cold math. To land 4 clients in 90 days you need on the order of 300–400 quality touches — which is exactly the 10–15/day routine above.

## The first-3-clients playbook

- Price **cheap-but-not-free** (e.g. 50–60% of list price — list prices in [Pricing](01-business-model-and-pricing.md)).
- In exchange, agree *in writing before starting*: a **testimonial**, a **case study** with numbers (hours saved, response time before/after), and **one referral introduction**.
- Over-deliver on these three. They are your marketing engine for the next twenty clients.

## Portfolio before clients exist

Build 2–3 public demos in week 1–2 (see [Roadmap](06-roadmap-90-days.md)): a support chatbot on a fake-but-realistic store, a lead-qualifier on a real-estate form, an invoice-to-sheet extractor. Record each as a 2–3 minute Loom. These are your proof until real case studies replace them.

## The one-page website

Needs exactly: outcome headline for your niche · 3 demo videos · 3 services with "from $X" prices · your face and name (trust) · one call-to-action (book a call / WhatsApp button). Skip at the start: blog, multi-page site, logo design, company name agonizing. A Carrd/Framer one-pager in a day is enough.

## Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | Outreach block (15 touches) + follow-ups; plan delivery week |
| Tue–Thu | Delivery mornings; 30–60 min outreach + calls each afternoon |
| Fri | Publish 1 demo/artifact; pipeline review; invoice/admin; 1h learning |

Non-negotiable: **outreach happens every day, even mid-project.** The famous solo-founder death spiral — deliver, finish, realize the pipeline is empty — is a scheduling failure, not a market failure.
