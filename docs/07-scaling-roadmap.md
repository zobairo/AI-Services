# Scaling Roadmap — Phases 2–5 (Months 4–36)

Continues from the [90-Day Roadmap](06-roadmap-90-days.md) (Phase 1). Each phase has entry conditions — **do not start a phase early because it looks more exciting than selling.** Money model per phase: [Financial Projections](09-financial-projections.md).

## Phase 2 — Full catalog (months 4–9)

**Entry condition (from Phase 1):** $2–5k/mo run rate, 3–5 retainers, 2+ case studies, template library started.
**Exit target:** $8–12k/mo run rate, $3–5k MRR, 8–12 retainer clients, VA hired, 2 new service categories live.

### Months 4–5 — Deepen
- [ ] Raise build prices 25–50% again (second raise); update the one-pager and site
- [ ] Add **voice services (A2, A5)**: pick the voice platform, build the demo clinic/restaurant receptionist, record the Loom — voice demos close hard
- [ ] Launch **F4 automation rescue** (a landing section + outreach angle: "did an AI project let you down?")
- [ ] Systemize delivery: written runbook per template; every delivery must end as an exported blueprint
- [ ] Start the upsell habit: every monthly report includes one paid improvement idea ([05](05-operations-legal.md))

### Months 6–7 — Leverage
- [ ] **Hire a VA** (part-time, $400–800/mo): outreach research, list building, scheduling, report assembly. You buy back ~10 h/week of selling/building time
- [ ] Add **marketing services (B1–B4)** to the public menu (you've likely done them ad hoc already)
- [ ] Sign **first white-label agency (F5)**: pitch 10 web/marketing agencies with the E6 one-pager
- [ ] Run your **first paid workshop (F3)** — even small; it produces leads and authority
- [ ] Client count check: if delivery eats outreach time completely → raise prices, don't add hours

### Months 8–9 — Package
- [ ] Build the first two **industry packages** ([08](08-industry-packages.md)) from your real delivered work — packages are discovered, not invented
- [ ] Add **document services (C1, C4)** to the menu; accounting firms are the volume buyers
- [ ] Publish case study #3 and #4 with numbers
- [ ] **Phase gate:** $8k+/mo, $3k+ MRR, VA productive, 1 agency partner active → Phase 3. If not: diagnose (pricing? niche? capacity?) and extend Phase 2 — do NOT hire builders yet

## Phase 3 — Boutique agency (months 10–18)

**Entry condition:** Phase 2 gate passed and you personally are the bottleneck (demand exists you can't deliver).
**Exit target:** $15–25k/mo, $8–12k MRR, 15–25 retainer clients, 1–2 contractor builders delivering with you at ≥40% margin after their cost, packages = majority of new deals.

### Team
- [ ] **Contractor builder #1** (project-based first, then retainer): delivers from your templates and runbooks; you review everything for the first 5 projects
- [ ] Quality control becomes a system: acceptance test cases are non-negotiable; you sign off every delivery ([05](05-operations-legal.md))
- [ ] Your calendar shifts to: sales calls, architecture/scoping, client relationships, QC — building only the new/hard things
- [ ] Builder #2 only when #1 is >80% booked for 6+ weeks

### Offer
- [ ] **Packages become the lead offer** — the website sells E1–E5, custom work becomes the exception at premium prices
- [ ] Add remaining C and D services; launch **F2 strategy retainers** for bigger clients
- [ ] 2–3 active agency partners (E6); partner revenue target ≥20% of new business
- [ ] Kill list: drop services and packages that didn't sell in 2 quarters

### System
- [ ] Proper CRM discipline (every lead, every stage), monthly finance review (margin per client, delivery hours per client — contractors make sloppy pricing fatal)
- [ ] Client concentration check quarterly: no client >40% ([00 principles](00-masterplan.md))
- [ ] **Phase gate:** $15k+/mo, $8k+ MRR, delivery running at target margin without your hands on every build → Phase 4

## Phase 4 — Productize (months 18–24)

**Entry condition:** Phase 3 gate passed; template library mature; you can see from sales data which automation is bought again and again.
**Exit target:** products ≥20% of revenue; a validated decision (yes or no, with data) on a SaaS spin-off.

- [ ] **Blueprints (F6):** package the 5 most-delivered templates as documented digital products ($50–500): workflow export + prompts + setup guide + video. Sell to DIY businesses and other freelancers/agencies
- [ ] **Productized delivery:** the best-selling package gets a fixed onboarding form, fixed timeline, fixed price — sellable without a sales call
- [ ] **SaaS decision:** the spin-off candidate is the automation clients ask for most with the least customization (history says: booking agents, review automation, or speed-to-lead). Validate with 10 paying design partners from your client base before writing product code. **The services business keeps funding everything — don't starve it for the product dream**
- [ ] Partner network: recruit 2–3 more white-label agencies; consider a certified-setup program for your blueprints
- [ ] **Launch F9 (course & community):** turn your delivery know-how into a "build AI automations for local businesses" course + paid community for other builders — they buy blueprints, become certified partners, and market you for free
- [ ] **AI-Employee subscriptions (F7) at full lineup:** all three plans live, sold without sales calls from the website
- [ ] Year-2 review against [projections](09-financial-projections.md); enter Phase 5 with data

## Phase 5 — Scale or spin off (months 24–36)

**Entry condition:** Phase 4 gate passed; you have 24 months of data on what sells, what retains, and what you enjoy running.
**This phase is a decision, then execution of that decision.** Three valid paths — pick with the year-2 numbers, not with mood:

### Path A — Grow the agency
For you if: packages + subscriptions sell steadily, delivery margin holds ≥50% with contractors, you like leading people.
- [ ] Hire: 2–4 builders, 1 delivery lead (your QC clone), part-time salesperson on commission
- [ ] Open a second vertical market or a second geography/language market
- [ ] Target: $25–60k/mo, you fully out of delivery, EBITDA ≥35%
- [ ] Optional: this is the shape buyers acquire — recurring base + templates + team = a sellable asset

### Path B — All-in on the product
For you if: one blueprint/SaaS candidate shows pull (10+ paying design partners, low-churn, low-custom demand).
- [ ] Keep a slimmed service arm (top 2 packages only) as funding + customer-discovery engine
- [ ] Build the product on the validated automation; your retainer clients are the beta base and first testimonials
- [ ] Target: product MRR ≥ service MRR by month 36; raise or bootstrap deliberately, not by accident

### Path C — Lean profitable boutique
For you if: $15–25k/mo with 1 contractor and mostly-recurring revenue is the life you actually want.
- [ ] Prune to the highest-margin 60% of clients and 2 packages; raise prices on the rest until they leave or become highest-margin too
- [ ] Automate your own ops with your own catalog (you are also a client)
- [ ] Target: same revenue, fewer hours, ≥60% net margin — a calm, durable business

- [ ] **Month-36 review:** whichever path — check against [projections](09-financial-projections.md) year-3 scenarios and reset the 3-year horizon

## Standing rules across all phases

1. **The daily outreach rhythm never stops** — it only delegates (VA researches, you touch).
2. **Every phase gate is data, not feelings.** Miss the gate → extend the phase, fix the constraint.
3. **Hire only against proven overflow demand**, never ahead of it.
4. **Templates before headcount:** if a process isn't documented, hiring for it multiplies chaos.
5. **Raise prices at every gate.**
